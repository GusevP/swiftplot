## Features

A feature is a function you import from `swiftplot` and list in `use`:

```js
import { legend, plot, tooltip, zoom } from 'swiftplot';

plot(el, {
  ...options,
  use: [legend(), tooltip({ mode: 'nearest', text: label => label }), zoom()],
});
```

`use` is read once, when the chart is created. `update()` does not take it: to add or drop a feature, create the
chart again, or in React and Vue change the component's `key`.
[Updates and animation](/guides/updates-and-animation#change-the-features) shows how.

In Vue, `onHiddenChange`, `onViewChange` and `onPointerChange` below arrive as the `update:hidden`, `update:view` and
`update:pointer` events, so `v-model:hidden` and the like work, and `onClick`, `onLayout` and `onHover` as `click`,
`layout` and `hover`.

## Legend and hidden series

`legend()` lists each labelled series above the plot. Pointing at an entry focuses its series, and a click, a tap,
Enter or Space hides or shows it; `legend({ toggle: false })` turns that off. A label wider than the plot ends in an
ellipsis. The rows of entries stop at what fits in a third of the chart's height, or at `legend({ rows: 2 })`, and the
rest go behind "+N more".

<GuideExample id="guide-plot" />

`hidden: ['API']` leaves out the series a legend lists under that id, which is a mark's `group`, or its `label`.
`onHiddenChange(hidden)` reports each change the legend makes. An automatic domain fits the series shown; `fit: 'all'`
on an axis holds it still.

A series is focused while the pointer is on its legend entry, or on the series itself with `tooltip()` in `use`. Focus
shows only with `dim` below 1: `dim: 0.3` draws the other series at that share of their opacity meanwhile.

## Tooltip

`tooltip()` comes in two modes:

- `{ mode: 'nearest', text }` shows what is nearest the pointer, within 20 px. `text(label, x, y, row)` gets the label
  of the mark hit, its x and y, and its row, so you can read more values from your own columns.
- `{ mode: 'x', title, value }` shows a row for each labelled line and area at the pointer's x, under a crosshair.
  `title(x)` writes the first line, and `value(y, axis)` each series' value, told whether it is on `y` or `y2`.

The candlesticks below are a `candlestickChart`, which comes with a tooltip in the nearest mode: it writes the date of the
candle and its four prices.

On a touch screen a tap shows the tooltip. It stays after the finger lifts, until a tap elsewhere or the chart's next
update or resize.

## Zoom and pan

`zoom()` zooms x about the pointer on ctrl or cmd and the wheel, or a trackpad pinch, and pans on a drag. A double click
or a double tap shows the whole domain again. On a touch screen two fingers pinch and pan, and one scrolls the page.

- `axes: 'y'` or `'xy'` zooms other axes than x.
- `wheel: 'plain'` zooms on a plain wheel, and `wheel: false` not on the wheel at all.
- `drag: 'shift'` pans only with shift held.

The window shown is the `view` option, `{ x?: [lo, hi], y?: [lo, hi], y2?: [lo, hi] }` in axis values.
`onViewChange(view)` reports it after a gesture, never after `update()`. With `fit: 'window'` on the y axis, y fits
what the x window shows.

## Brush

`brush()` reports a range. A drag draws a rectangle, and `onSelect` gets the range in axis values on release. The
rectangle stays until the next press on the plot, a zoom or pan, a resize or change of pixel density, a hidden series,
or an update of anything but `pointer` and the callbacks, and `onSelect` then gets `null`. `brush({ zoom: true })`
instead shows the range brushed and drops the rectangle at once, with no `null` after it. With `zoom()` beside it, a
plain drag brushes and shift and a drag pans. A brush takes the mouse and a pen only.

## Clicks and the pointer

`onClick(hit, at)` gets what is within 20 px of a click on the plot, or `null`, and the axis values at the click. A
click that ends a drag is not one. `selected: [{ id: 'API', rows: [3] }]` picks rows out: they stay at full strength
and, while nothing is focused, the other rows are drawn at `dim`. Bars and dots outline the rows picked. A line or an
area is one shape, so picking any of its rows draws the whole of it at full strength.

`pointer: { x, y }` makes `tooltip()` show as if the user's pointer were there, and `onPointerChange` reports the
user's, with `null` as it leaves. Two charts can follow each other that way:

```ts
const charts = [a, b].map((el, i) =>
  plot(el, {
    ...options,
    onPointerChange: p => charts[1 - i]!.update({ pointer: p }),
    use: [tooltip(tip)],
  }),
);
```

## Drawing over the chart

`chart.overlay` is an element over the whole chart, under its tooltip, for your own DOM: markers, labels, or a tooltip of
your own with `tooltip()` left out. `onLayout` and `onHover` say where to put it, in CSS pixels from the chart's top left
corner.

- `onLayout(layout)` gets `{ width, height, margin, frame }`, and on a chart with axes what each shows, `x`, `y` and `y2`
  as `{ domain, ticks }`, and the functions `xPx(x)`, `yPx(y, axis)`, `xValue(px)` and `yValue(px, axis)`, all in the
  axes' own values. It is called after the first paint and whenever the geometry changes, and not while the chart has no
  room. Each layout is a new frozen object and keeps converting by its own geometry.
- `onHover(hover)` gets `{ px, py, hit }` as the pointer moves over the plot, and again when a zoom, new data or a resize
  changes what is under it at rest. `hit` is the one `onClick` gets, with the `px` and `py` it is anchored at, such as a
  dot's centre; it is `null` with nothing within 20 px, and a line or an area answers no pointer. `onHover(null)` comes once
  as the pointer leaves the plot or a drag starts.

The first `onLayout` runs inside `plot()`, before you have the chart. To place something in `chart.overlay` from it, take
the chart first and set it with `update()`, which calls it with the current layout:

```js
const chart = plot(el, options);
const marker = chart.overlay.appendChild(document.createElement('span'));
const tip = chart.overlay.appendChild(document.createElement('div'));
marker.style.position = tip.style.position = 'absolute';
marker.textContent = 'Target';

chart.update({
  onLayout({ xPx, yPx }) {
    marker.style.left = `${xPx(20)}px`;
    marker.style.top = `${yPx(50)}px`;
  },
  onHover(hover) {
    const hit = hover?.hit;
    tip.hidden = !hit;
    if (!hit) return;
    tip.textContent = `${hit.x}, ${hit.y}`;
    tip.style.left = `${hit.px}px`;
    tip.style.top = `${hit.py}px`;
  },
});
```

The charts from data take both callbacks and have the same `overlay`, which stays, with its children, when a changed
feature creates the chart again. [Your own markers and tooltip](/examples/overlay-tooltip) is such a chart.

Either callback may set the options that draw the chart without looping: data or marks made anew with equal values report
nothing. An axis's `to` and `from` must stay the same functions, though. `onLayout` compares them by identity, so an axis
that makes them in each update, set from `onLayout`, would report without end. `log()` already keeps them.

The overlay lets the pointer through. A child you give `pointer-events: auto`, such as a button, takes it:

- `onHover`, `onPointerChange` and the built-in tooltip go on over it as over the plot. The cursor is its own, and a touch
  on it reports no hover.
- A click, the wheel, a drag, a brush, a double click or a pinch on it is its own and does not reach the chart. A pan begun
  on the plot goes on across it.
- While it is pressed and the pointer stays in the chart, no hover is reported and the last one stays, so an element you
  show from `onHover` is not taken away under the press. A pointer that leaves the chart, or whose press the browser
  cancels, reports `null` as usual, and new data of the hovered mark reports what is then under it. The
  built-in tooltip hides until the mouse or pen is released.
