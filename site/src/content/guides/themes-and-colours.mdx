## Themes

A theme holds the chart's own colours, not the data's:

- `text` for the axis text and the legend;
- `muted` for the tick marks;
- `grid` for the grid lines;
- `surface` and `border` for the tooltip's box. `surface` is also the middle of a ringed point.

`swiftplot` exports two, `light` and `dark`. `plot()` takes `light` unless its `theme` is given, and never guesses dark
mode: the app picks the theme. Series colours belong to the marks, so the app picks those too. `paletteDark` has the
same eight hues as `palette`, each with a contrast of 3:1 on `dark.surface`.

```ts
import { dark, light, line, palette, paletteDark, plot } from 'swiftplot';

const scheme = matchMedia('(prefers-color-scheme: dark)');
const themed = (isDark: boolean) => ({
  theme: isDark ? dark : light,
  marks: [line({ x: day, y: api, color: (isDark ? paletteDark : palette)[0]!, label: 'API' })],
});
const chart = plot(el, themed(scheme.matches));
scheme.addEventListener('change', e => chart.update(themed(e.matches)));
```

A theme is a plain object, so your own is one too, such as `theme: { ...dark, grid: '#262626' }`. Replacing it with
another object repaints the chart and restyles its text.

The charts from data, in `swiftplot/charts`, pick for themselves. Their `theme` defaults to `'auto'`, which follows the
computed CSS `color-scheme` of the chart's element, word by word: `dark` without `light`, as in `dark` or `only dark`,
gives the dark theme; both, in either order, follow the system setting and switch with it; no `dark` gives light. It is
read when the chart is created and when the `theme` prop changes. Only a chart that found both also listens to the
system setting, and reads it again when that changes; a chart that found a fixed scheme keeps it until its `theme` prop
changes. `'light'`, `'dark'` or a theme object fixes it. Their `colors` default to `palette`, or `paletteDark` on the
dark theme.

The React and Vue components of those charts do the same. `Plot` is `plot()`: it is light unless its `theme` is given,
and does not follow the page.

The charts on this site follow the theme switch in the top bar. On a dark page they take the dark theme and the site's
own dark colours, and `paletteDark` where the code uses `palette`.

## Series colours

`palette` is eight colours for eight series, to be given out in order. The order keeps neighbours apart for
colour-blind readers, and each colour has a contrast of 3:1 on white.

A treemap or a sunburst gives each root a colour from `colors`, or each child of a single root. The sunburst below has
four roots and takes `palette`; Colours gives it this site's three colours and an amber instead. The rings under a root
are lighter steps of its colour, and Shade replaces them with one run from the full colour at the centre to that
opacity at the rim.

## Gradients

`gradient(from, to, toward)` blends two stops in a direction on the screen: `'down'`, `'up'`, `'right'` or `'left'`,
and `'down'` when left out. A stop is a CSS colour, or a number for the mark's own colour at that opacity, so
`gradient(0.4, 0)` fades a mark's colour out downwards. `brand` is the cyan-to-violet blend, from left to right.

A blend goes in `area({ fill })`, `bar({ fill })`, `box({ fill })`, `line({ stroke })` and `line({ fill })`.
`line({ fill: true })` is a faint fade of the line's own colour under it. `color` stays the one colour that a legend
and a tooltip show.

```js
import { area, gradient } from 'swiftplot';

const marks = [area({ x: day, y: used, color: '#0891b2', label: 'Used', fill: gradient(0.4, 0) })];
```

[The annotated line](/examples/line-annotated) fills under its line this way.

## Other blends

- `sankey({ blend: true })` runs each ribbon from its source's colour into its target's.
- `funnel({ fill: gradient('#0891b2', '#9333ea') })` puts one blend down the whole funnel.
- `arcs({ sweep: 0.8 })` and `radialBars({ sweep: 0.35 })` start each slice or bar at that opacity and bring it to full
  colour at its end. They stay solid where the browser has no conic gradient, and a pie that draws more than 64 slices
  stays solid too.
- `treemap({ shade: 0.7 })` starts each branch at that opacity at its top, and `sunburst({ shade: 0.7 })` ends each at
  that opacity at the rim.
- `radar({ fill })` is the opacity of each series' filling, 0.15 when left out.

## A colour scale

A heatmap colours its cells through a colour scale, `sequential({ stops, domain })`. The values run through the stops,
which are `#rgb` or `#rrggbb` colours, pale to dark blue when left out, from the smallest value to the largest unless
`domain` fixes the ends. `legend()` shows the scale as a strip of its colours between the ends of its domain. Create the
scale once, outside the update, or every update repaints the grid.
